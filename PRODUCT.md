## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary: the C-suite of startups and growing companies** — chief executives and
chief financial officers, plus founders acting in both roles. They have recurring,
high-stakes communications needs and little or no in-house design function.

Two jobs, deliberately separated on the site:

- **CEO** — needs the story to win: investor decks, board narratives, keynotes,
  positioning. Their situation is a room where a decision gets made in seconds.
- **CFO** — needs capability without a headcount line. Their situation is a
  budget conversation about a recurring cost with unpredictable shape.

**How they arrive: outbound-led, but must hold up cold.** The common path is a
named person receiving a link — typically an audience page — from someone at
Gravino. The home page still has to work unaccompanied for someone who finds
the firm through search or referral. Design for a considered read; do not
assume accompaniment.

## Product Purpose

Gravino is the communications capability a company does not have the headcount
to hire. It covers the full surface of how a business communicates — investor
deck to launch film to internal playbook — as one embedded senior team that
learns the business once and then handles all of it.

Success is a prospect sending a real deck for the free teardown.

## Positioning

**The third option, between freelancers and an in-house hire.** Freelancers give
one format each, fragmenting the brand across many hands; an in-house hire is a
fixed cost for demand that arrives in waves and is capped by one person's range.
Gravino is a senior team, covering every format, commissioned as needed.

The claim a neighbouring studio could not truthfully copy is the *combination*:
senior craft, full-surface coverage, and cost that scales with need, under one
point of contact. Most studios do identity; most freelancers do one artefact.

## Operating Context

The work lives in high-stakes rooms and the documents that survive them: seed
rounds through IPO roadshows, board meetings, ESG and annual reporting, launch
campaigns, sales enablement, event and physical presence.

Engagements run across **US, Europe, Gulf and India**, from **Chennai, India**.
Engagement shapes are project-based when scope is defined and retainer when the
work is continuous.

## Capabilities and Constraints

**Ten disciplines in four groups**, organised by the outcome each produces:
capital & corporate narrative (3), brand & identity systems (2), growth &
digital marketing (3), public & physical experience (2).

Team: a senior **4-person** core, **75+ years** combined experience.

Confirmed commercial terms: scope fixed before work begins; a clear quote after
a short conversation; client files and full copyright transfer on completion;
what the client shares stays confidential.

Constraints:

- **No pricing is published.** A deliberate reversal of an earlier decision to
  show "starting from" figures. Quotes follow a conversation.
- **No backend.** Contact is a `mailto:` today. A form that silently goes
  nowhere is worse than an address that works.
- Existing stack answers the build question: Next.js 15, TypeScript,
  Tailwind v4, deployed to `gravino.in`.

## Brand Commitments

**Binding, and not to be altered:**

- Wordmark "Grav" + the bubble carrying "ino" + the satellite dot. "ino" is set
  at the same size as "Grav" — one word, and the bubble is a container around
  the second half.
- Tagline: **Where Balance Meets Value**. Lockup line: **Value Has Gravity.**
- The blue-violet-blush glass world and the three photoreal renders.
- Brochure typefaces are **Hagrid** (display) and **DM Sans** (body). Hagrid is
  commercial and its webfont licence is **unconfirmed**; the site currently
  substitutes Instrument Sans.

**Latitude, confirmed with the user:** the brochure is the *floor*, not the
ceiling. Logo, tagline, renders and palette are fixed; layout, scale,
typography, motion and overall loudness may go further than the brochure does.
The brochure is print — the site may do what print cannot.

Voice: transparent, senior, plain. Claims are specific and falsifiable rather
than superlative.

## Product Principles

1. **Credibility is the product.** The site argues that Gravino makes businesses
   legible and trustworthy; anything invented on it refutes that argument.
   Absent evidence stays absent rather than becoming plausible filler.
2. **One team, one contact, every format.** Coverage and single accountability
   are the differentiator — structure should always make the breadth visible.
3. **Two audiences, two arguments.** The CEO case is narrative and outcome; the
   CFO case is cost shape and accountability. Never blend them into one voice.
4. **Specific over superlative.** The comparison table converts because it is
   falsifiable. Prefer claims that could be checked.
5. **Low-friction first step.** The teardown offer — send a deck, get a page
   back, no pitch — is the primary conversion everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific standard has been established by the user. Baseline
obligations apply: the comparison table must remain a real table with row
headers, the live WebGL scenes are decorative and must not carry meaning, and
motion should respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
